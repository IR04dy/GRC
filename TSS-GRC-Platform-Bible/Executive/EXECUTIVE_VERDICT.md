# Executive Verdict

## Board-level opinion

**Invest, but do not market the current build as a fully assured, independently modular enterprise GRC suite.**

TSS GRC is a high-potential Saudi product asset hidden inside a high-complexity software estate. It demonstrates more local domain breadth than many early-stage competitors: NCA, SAMA, PDPL, BCM, cyber operations, ESG, green finance, access governance, Sharia, open banking and regulatory processes. The same breadth now works against the product:

- navigation reflects hundreds of implementation surfaces;
- core objects are duplicated or weakly reconciled;
- some important governance decisions can be authored under caller-supplied identities;
- commercial entitlements do not consistently protect backend capability;
- module separation is not an installable-product property;
- documentation sometimes confuses “implemented” with “safe, usable and sellable”.

## Brutal conclusion

The platform is **feature-rich but not yet trust-rich**. A sophisticated buyer will be impressed in the first demonstration and concerned during technical due diligence. A government acceptance committee will ask for actor provenance, tenant isolation, accessibility, backup restoration, Saudi hosting and end-to-end evidence. Several answers are currently documents or intentions rather than retained proof.

## What should not be done

- Do not add another major module before stabilising the platform kernel.
- Do not split immediately into microservices.
- Do not sell “all modules independently” until backend entitlements and dependency manifests are enforceable.
- Do not claim regulatory compliance; sell compliance operations, evidence and traceability.
- Do not equate 2,316 green tests with acceptance readiness.
- Do not retain hundreds of routes in one undifferentiated navigation model.

## Strategic advantage

The winning position is:

> **The Arabic-first Saudi assurance operating system: one evidence graph, configurable regulatory packs, and independently purchasable GRC workspaces.**

This avoids competing only on global feature volume. It competes on Saudi time-to-value, Arabic decision quality, regulator-ready evidence and composable deployment.

## 12-month outcome

The transformation succeeds when:

1. the kernel can run with no commercial modules installed;
2. each package has an entitlement, dependency manifest, isolated navigation, API surface, schema ownership and uninstall policy;
3. forged actors, fake quorum and cross-tenant access fail automatically;
4. five flagship journeys pass Arabic usability and WCAG 2.2 AA testing;
5. deployment, backup restoration and upgrade are rehearsed on a Saudi-region reference architecture;
6. sales can demonstrate value in 30 minutes using role-specific outcomes, not tab tours;
7. three lighthouse Saudi customers validate product-market fit.

## Investment sequence

| Sequence | Investment | Why now |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Governance trust boundary | Invalid decisions destroy buyer trust |
| 2 | Platform kernel and package enforcement | Required for independent module sales |
| 3 | UX simplification and Arabic design system | Converts capability into adoption |
| 4 | Evidence graph and common object vocabulary | Reduces duplicated compliance work |
| 5 | Saudi content operations | Makes local differentiation sustainable |
| 6 | AI copilots with human accountability | Adds leverage after the data/control foundation is reliable |
