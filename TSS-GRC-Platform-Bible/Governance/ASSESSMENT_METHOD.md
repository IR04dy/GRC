# Assessment Method

## Scope

The baseline combines repository archaeology, architecture analysis, code and schema inspection, automated verification, UX information-architecture inspection, business-process review and official Saudi reference research.

This is an independent product and engineering opinion. It is not legal advice, a penetration test, a WCAG conformance audit, a regulator certification or a production operational-resilience test.

Scores measure demonstrated maturity, not feature count.

## Review lenses

Every capability is challenged through nine lenses:

1. buyer outcome and willingness to pay;
2. GRC control design and three-lines accountability;
3. bounded-context ownership;
4. data authority and evidence provenance;
5. authenticated authority and abuse resistance;
6. Arabic-first task usability and accessibility;
7. independent packaging and lifecycle;
8. Saudi regulatory relevance;
9. operability and total cost of ownership.

## Competitive comparison rule

Competitor references are pattern comparisons, not claims of exact feature parity. TSS should copy neither legacy complexity nor marketing language. It should learn from:

- ServiceNow: common data, continuous monitoring and workflow reach;
- Archer and MetricStream: configurable risk taxonomies and enterprise accountability;
- OneTrust: purpose-built privacy/third-party workflows and a shared trust data model;
- AuditBoard: connected audit-risk-compliance experience;
- Atlassian: approachable intake, queues, automation and change/deployment context;
- Microsoft and Salesforce: managed solution/package lifecycle and declarative dependencies.
